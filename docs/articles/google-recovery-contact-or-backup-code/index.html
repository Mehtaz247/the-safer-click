Google Recovery Contacts and Backup Codes Do Different Jobs

Set up a recovery contact for account-recovery help, and keep backup codes for a missing 2-Step Verification method.

Published November 20, 2026Updated November 24, 20263 min read

The short version

  • A recovery contact can help when you forget your password or cannot sign in for another reason; it is not a set of sign-in codes.
  • Backup codes are one-time 2-Step Verification codes for when you cannot receive your normal second step.
  • Choose a trusted recovery contact and store backup codes somewhere private and separate from the phone or account they may need to help replace.

Next evidence review: March 24, 2027

Choose the fallback for the problem

A recovery contact helps with Google Account recovery. A backup code supplies the second step when normal 2-Step Verification is unavailable. Neither promises that every lockout can be resolved. Our recommendation is to prepare the options you can maintain, rather than collecting settings you do not understand.

This guide covers personal Google Accounts. Recovery options vary by region, account and device. Work or school accounts may need administrator help. These instructions follow current Google documentation; they are not a report of testing your account.

Recovery contact: prepare a trusted person

Google says to choose someone you know well and trust. From Google Account, open Security & sign-in, then Recovery contacts under How you sign in to Google. Add the person and send the request. The invitation shares your name, email address and profile photo.

The request expires after seven days. After acceptance, another seven-day period applies before recovery use. Google says a recovery contact does not receive your security alerts or notifications. You cannot add recovery information while locked out or already in the recovery process.

Our practical check: discuss the invitation first, then revisit the setting to confirm acceptance. Decide how you will contact that person without relying only on the account you might lose. Do not send passwords or backup codes as part of this conversation.

Backup codes: prepare a private second step

Google backup codes require 2-Step Verification. In Google Account, choose Security & sign-in, then 2-Step Verification and Backup codes. Continue to the controls for generating, printing or downloading a set. Advanced Protection Program users cannot download these codes.

Each code works once. Generating a fresh set of ten invalidates the previous set. Google recommends storing a printed copy safely and says not to share codes. It asks for a backup code only during sign-in.

When needed, sign in through Google directly, select Try another way, then Enter one of your 8-digit backup codes. Use an unused code and mark it used. If a set is lost or suspected stolen, generate a replacement set.

Run a preparation check without exposing secrets

Our checklist is to inspect the settings while you still have normal access, confirm the contact accepted, and confirm that saved codes can be reached without the device they are meant to replace. You do not need to send anyone a code to prove that the set exists.

Revisit the plan after changing a number, replacing a device or losing contact with your helper. If you are already locked out, use Google’s official account-recovery route and the options it offers; this guide cannot shorten a waiting period or guarantee restoration.

  1. Check which recovery options your account actually offers.
  2. Confirm contact acceptance and allow the documented waiting period.
  3. Store unused backup codes privately and keep track of replacements.
  4. Keep a way to reach your helper without the affected account.
