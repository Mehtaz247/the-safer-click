An Unwanted Event Appeared in Your Calendar. What Now?

Separate a suspicious invitation from an unwanted calendar subscription, then use the calendar's own controls rather than the event's links.

Published November 27, 2026Updated November 27, 20263 min read

The short version

  • Open your calendar independently and identify the event or calendar you want to remove.
  • Reporting an event, changing future invitation settings, and unsubscribing from a calendar do different jobs.
  • Check the scope before removing a whole calendar that may contain events you need.

Next evidence review: March 27, 2027

Start with the calendar, not the event's demand

Our recommendation is to treat a surprising calendar entry as something to inspect, not an instruction to pay, sign in through its link, or call its number. Open the calendar from your usual app or trusted address. If the invitation claims to come from someone you know, check with them through an established channel before acting on the request.

This guide covers Google Calendar on a computer and Apple's Calendar app on iPhone with iOS 26 or later. It is based on provider documentation, not testing your account. Other apps and older versions can have different controls. Managed Google accounts may have invitation settings restricted by an administrator.

Google Calendar: report the event already present

On a computer, open Google Calendar, select the suspicious event, then More actions and Report as spam. Google says reporting removes the event; for a recurring event, it removes the whole series.

Google's reporting option applies to events sent from Google Calendar. Events created by another provider, app or service cannot be reported this way. If the control is unavailable, check which service supplied the event and use that provider's documented help. Do not assume every calendar app displays the same menu.

Google Calendar: choose how future invitations appear

For future invitations, open Settings, then Event settings under General, and Add invitations to my calendar. Google offers Only if the sender is known, which automatically adds events from contacts, people in your organization, or people you previously interacted with.

Another option is When I respond to the invitation in email. Google says this adds the event only after your response. With the known-sender option, invitations that are not added still arrive by email. These settings affect new invitations; they do not clean up old events.

Choose the tradeoff deliberately. Google warns that Only if the sender is known might reveal to senders that they are not in your contacts. Our suggestion is to review legitimate pending invitations after changing the setting, so a quieter calendar does not make you overlook a meeting you intended to attend.

iPhone: distinguish an event from a whole calendar

Apple's instructions for iOS 26 or later say to tap an unwanted event, choose Delete Event, and confirm. For an event from an unknown contact, the documented reporting route is Report Junk, then Delete and Report Junk.

If the problem is an unwanted calendar subscription, open Calendar, tap Calendars, then the information button beside that calendar. Choose Delete Calendar or Unsubscribe and confirm. Check the calendar's name and contents before confirming a change to the whole calendar.

Apple also lets you hide a calendar by deselecting its checkmark in the Calendars list. Some calendars cannot be removed and can instead be hidden. Hiding and unsubscribing are separate controls; choose the one that matches your intended result.

Check the result without following the suspicious link

Our final check is to return to the calendar list, confirm which event or subscription changed, and review any legitimate invitations waiting for attention. Removing one unwanted event is not a reason to approve a new subscription or trust another message from its sender.

If you already entered a password, installed software or sent money through the event's instructions, removing the entry does not address those actions. Use the relevant provider's official recovery help through a trusted route.

  1. Identify the event's provider and the calendar containing it.
  2. Use the documented report, delete or unsubscribe control.
  3. Review future invitation settings separately.
  4. Confirm the result and check legitimate pending invitations.
