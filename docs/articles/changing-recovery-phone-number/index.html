Changing Your Phone Number? Check Account Recovery First

Updating a contact number and replacing an account recovery method are separate jobs. Google and Microsoft also apply different waiting periods.

Published November 20, 2026Updated November 20, 20263 min read

The short version

  • Review recovery settings while you can still sign in and control the old number.
  • Do not assume a recovery change takes effect immediately or removes every use of that number.
  • An account change you did not request needs a security review, not passive waiting.

Next evidence review: March 20, 2027

Make an account-by-account plan

Our recommendation: before giving up an old number, list the important accounts that use it and check each account's recovery settings through its official app or a trusted address. Avoid making several irreversible changes at once. Keep the plan free of passwords and verification codes.

This guide covers personal Google Accounts and Microsoft accounts. It does not establish the rules for a bank, mobile carrier, workplace, or school. Google notes that recovery options vary by region, account, and device, and managed accounts may need administrator help. The steps below come from provider documentation; we have not tested your account.

Google: change recovery details before a lockout

Google says you cannot add recovery information while locked out or going through account recovery. While signed in, open your Google Account, select Security & sign-in, then Recovery phone under How you sign in to Google. Follow the displayed editing steps. Use a number that belongs only to you and that you regularly use.

Google says a new or changed recovery phone can take up to seven days to take effect. Codes may still go to previous recovery information for seven days after a change. Removing a recovery phone also does not necessarily remove its use in other Google services. Review those uses separately. See Google's linked recovery-options guidance for the current controls.

Microsoft: read scheduled-removal notices carefully

Microsoft's guidance says a sign-in verification method scheduled for removal has a 30-day waiting period. That method can still verify sign-ins until its scheduled removal time. This is a rule about removing a verification method; do not assume every edit to every account has the same delay.

To cancel a removal, Microsoft directs you to the account's Additional security page: expand the verification option and choose Cancel removal. If you did not request the change, Microsoft says to cancel it, check for unfamiliar new verification methods, and follow its account-security guidance. Open the account independently rather than trusting a message's link.

Finish with a check, not an assumption

Our checklist is to confirm the number and recovery alternatives displayed by each provider, read any pending-change notice, and revisit the settings when the stated waiting period ends. A saved change is not by itself evidence that every old contact or verification route has disappeared.

If you have already lost access, use the provider's official recovery process or your established administrator contact. This planning guide cannot promise recovery or shorten a provider's waiting period.

  1. List accounts using the old number.
  2. Review each provider's recovery controls while signed in.
  3. Read and record pending-change dates without recording codes.
  4. Check the completed settings again after the provider's stated period.
